# Anatomy of a Brand Guideline

This is the reference model the skill uses. It was built by taking apart two
real guides: a 17-page 2025 visual-identity guide from a cloud security
company (lean, visual-first, logo and color heavy) and a 98-page 2019 guide
from an endpoint security company (comprehensive: voice, personas, visual
system, sub-brand architecture, co-branding, editorial style, checklist).
Between them they cover nearly every section a modern brand guideline needs,
and their mistakes are just as instructive as their strengths.

A brand guideline is not a design exercise. It is a **record of decisions
already made**, written so that someone who was not in the room (an agency,
a partner, a new hire, an AI design tool) can reproduce the brand correctly
without asking. Every section answers three questions:

1. **What is it?** (the asset or value, exactly specified)
2. **When and how do I use it?** (the approved applications)
3. **When must I not use it?** (the prohibited applications, shown visually)

If a section only answers question 1, it is a swatch library, not a guideline.

## 1. Front matter

| Element | Purpose | Source pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Cover | Signals the brand immediately. Logo, title, often a hero visual or mascot. | Both guides open on a dark, full-bleed brand moment. |
| Purpose statement | Who this document is for and how strict it is. | 2019 guide: "for internal employees as well as outside resources... creativity is encouraged within the context of the guidelines." This sets tone: rules are fixed, expression is flexible. |
| Version and date | Lets readers know if they hold the current copy. | 2019 guide prints `REV. 3.0. 20190426` on the cover. The 2025 guide has no version, which is a gap. |
| Table of contents | Navigation for anything over ~15 pages. | 2019 guide numbers sections 01 to 05. |
| Copyright and trademark notice | Legal protection of marks. | 2019 guide closes with a full trademark attribution block. |

## 2. Brand foundation (verbal)

This is the "why" that every visual rule ultimately protects.

- **Mission.** One sentence. The 2025 guide gives its mission its own full
  slide at the start and repeats it as the closing slide, which bookends the
  whole document with purpose. Strong pattern.
- **Mantra or tagline.** A short internal rallying idea (a two to four word idea the whole company repeats). Distinguish internal mantra from external tagline.
- **Positioning statement.** Who we are and what sets us apart.
- **Boilerplate.** The approved "About" paragraph for press releases and
  bios. Copy-paste ready. Often forgotten and always needed.
- **Brand pillars.** Three or so themes with supporting proof points
  ("Smart. Simple. Secure." each with bulleted proof).
- **Value proposition / framing.** What we say about ourselves vs. what we
  enable customers to do (2019 guide uses a two-column "We... / Allowing
  users to..." frame, which is a useful template).
- **Voice and tone.** Personality traits, plus explicit "we are / we are not"
  pairs. The 2019 guide encodes tone per audience ("be direct, absolutely no
  fear mongering, no fluff or jargon").

## 3. Audience (optional)

Personas belong in a brand guide only when they change how the brand speaks.
The 2019 guide includes two named personas (an executive buyer and a
practitioner), each with titles, goals, barriers, stresses, aspirations,
messaging considerations, preferred channels, and tone/approach rules, plus a
buyer's journey table. Useful for marketing teams, overkill for an external
partner kit. The skill treats this as optional and keeps it to tone guidance
per audience.

## 4. Logo system

The heart of every guide and the area with the most rules.

### 4.1 Primary logo
- Description of the parts: **logomark** (symbol), **logotype / wordmark**
  (lettering), **lockup** (the two combined in a fixed relationship).
- A sentence on what it represents and why it must not be altered ("weakens
  the integrity of the brand... and our legal protection").

### 4.2 Variations and when to use each
Both guides define variants by **background**, not by aesthetic preference:

| Variant | Typical rule |
|---|---|
| Full color on light ("light mode") | Default on white or light neutral backgrounds. |
| Knockout / reversed ("dark mode") | On black, dark, or brand-colored backgrounds. |
| All white | On saturated brand-color backgrounds. |
| All black / single color | Only when color reproduction is impossible or on specific light backgrounds. |
| Logomark only | When space does not allow the full logo (favicons, social avatars, app icons). |
| Stacked vs. horizontal | Stacked for square (1:1) constraints, horizontal everywhere else. The 2019 guide shows proof that stacked fails at small non-square sizes. |

A guide should label the **preferred** variant explicitly (2019: "green and
white on black is the preferred use").

### 4.3 Approved background pairings
Show each logo variant on each approved background color. Also show which
pairings are "acceptable but use minimally."

### 4.4 Clear space (exclusion zone)
- Define clear space as a **unit derived from the logo itself** (commonly the
  height of the mark, a letter, or a fraction of it), never as a fixed
  measurement, so it scales.
- Illustrate it with a box diagram.
- The 2025 guide adds a scaling rule: at favicon size the mark fills about
  90% of the canvas, at 128 px about 60%. Small sizes get proportionally
  less padding. Good, rarely documented detail.

### 4.5 Minimum size
- Print minimum in inches or millimeters and digital minimum in pixels, per
  variant. The 2019 guide gives four forms (0.37 in to 1.74 in) and notes the
  registered trademark symbol is dropped at minimum size.

### 4.6 Trademark symbol rules
Whether the ® or ™ always appears, where, and when it can be dropped.

### 4.7 Incorrect usage ("Don'ts")
Shown as a grid of visual examples, each marked with a red X and a one-line
caption. The union of both guides gives the canonical list:

- Do not stretch, squash, or distort proportions
- Do not rotate, angle, or skew
- Do not recolor or use non-approved colors
- Do not apply effects (drop shadow, glow, bevel, gradient, outline)
- Do not apply patterns or textures to the logo
- Do not alter the size relationship between mark and wordmark
- Do not separate the mark from the wordmark (except approved mark-only use)
- Do not rearrange or extract parts
- Do not redraw, recreate, or retype the logotype in another font
- Do not place on busy photography or backgrounds that reduce legibility
- Do not use the logo inside a sentence as a word
- Do not add taglines, messaging, or sub-brand text to the logo
- Do not crowd it (violating clear space)
- Do not use on unapproved or off-brand background images
- Do not use outdated versions of the logo

Brand-specific don'ts matter most because nobody else can predict them.
Examples from the sources: "do not show the logo next to photos of the animal the brand is named
after," "do not use the accent line on the standalone mark," and "always
include the parent-company emblem with the wordmark." The interview must
dig for these.

### 4.8 Lockups and extended logo family
Rules for product names, department names, and campaign names next to the
logo. The 2025 guide reserves its logo font exclusively for official lockups
and forbids teams from creating their own ("DO NOT create your own category
logo. Use the primary logo and put the category in the content."). The 2019
guide specifies exact construction: gap is 50% of the master logo gap,
product name in a named font at 30 pt with 500 tracking.

## 5. Brand architecture (optional)

How the master brand relates to products, sub-brands, programs, and
consumer lines. The 2019 guide uses four tiers with a tree diagram, product
shields, assigned product colors, and construction specs per tier, plus a
consumer sub-brand that "inherits the master system but uses a lighter
palette and specific photography" and forbids using enterprise imagery.
Include only when the company actually has more than one brand.

## 6. Color system

### 6.1 Palette tiers
| Tier | Role | Typical size |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | Logo colors and dominant brand presence | 2 to 4 |
| Secondary / accent | Differentiation, charts, CTAs, focal points | 2 to 6 |
| Neutrals | Backgrounds, borders, dividers, body copy, tables | 6 to 12 steps |
| Functional / semantic | Status, risk, success, warning, error | 3 to 6 |
| Gradients | Approved color pairs with angle and direction | 0 to 4 |

Each tier needs a **usage sentence**, not just swatches. The best lines in
the sources: "use primary colors for backgrounds and key visual areas,"
"pair the darkest blue with the lightest when contrast is needed," "warm
colors are for strategic focal points and actions like buttons," "the
tertiary palette is supportive and should be used as accents, not as the
foundation," "the brand neutral palette uses the same range of grays as the
product UI."

### 6.2 Color specifications per swatch
Every swatch needs: **name**, **HEX**, **RGB**, **CMYK**, **Pantone (PMS)**
if printed. Optional: role, and the light/dark mode it belongs to.
Memorable names help adoption (the 2019 guide names its eight grays after
science-fiction characters, which makes them easy to request by name).

### 6.3 Light mode and dark mode
The 2025 guide explicitly labels some blues "ADA Lightmode" and "ADA
Darkmode": the same brand hue adjusted so text meets accessibility contrast
on each background. A modern guide should define, per mode:
background, surface/card, primary text, secondary text, border, accent,
link, and which logo variant to use.

### 6.4 Proportion
Neither source shows a usage ratio, which is a gap. A guide should state
roughly how much of a layout each color occupies (for example 60% neutral,
30% primary, 10% accent) so people stop using accent colors as backgrounds.

### 6.5 Accessibility
State WCAG contrast for the main text/background pairings. Neither source
publishes ratios; the 2025 guide implies them via "ADA" swatches only.

### 6.6 Gradients
If used: the stops, the allowed angles, and forbidden directions (2019:
"45, 60, or -60 degrees, left to right or right to left; never straight up
and down").

## 7. Typography

- **Typefaces**: family, role (display, headline, body, UI, code), weights
  allowed, licensing source, and **fallback/system font** for tools that
  cannot load it (email, Google Slides, Canva without upload).
- **Specimen**: alphabet, numerals, and weights.
- **Case**: sentence case vs. title case vs. uppercase (2019 guide records a
  deliberate shift from uppercase to sentence case "for approachability").
- **Hierarchy**: eyebrow, H1 to H4, lead, body, quote, caption, each with
  family, weight, size, line height, letter spacing, and allowed colors. The
  2025 guide does this well for web.
- **Color rules for type**: "headlines may only be set in primary colors."
- **Reserved fonts**: a font kept exclusively for the logo or lockups.

## 8. Imagery, iconography, and graphic elements

- **Photography**: subject (real people, candid), light (natural), color
  and focus (bright, shallow depth of field), what to avoid (staged stock,
  hoodie hackers, etc.). Show approved and rejected examples.
- **Illustration / mascot**: when the mascot appears and when it does not.
- **Iconography**: style (line vs. solid), grid, stroke, allowed colors and
  gradients, size range (2019: 80 px to 400 px), and where the library lives.
- **Graphic devices / patterns**: textures, shapes, motifs, and rules.

## 9. Co-branding and partner use

Both guides treat this as its own section because partners misuse logos
more than employees do. Canonical rules:

- **Order**: our logo first in our materials; partner first in theirs.
- **Balance**: equal visual weight (1:1), sized optically not mathematically.
- **Alignment**: center-aligned horizontally or vertically.
- **Spacing**: a minimum gap (2019: 0.5 in / 72 px).
- **Separator**: whether a vertical rule, plus sign, or dotted rule is
  allowed. Note the sources **disagree**: one requires a divider, the other
  lists a dividing line as a don't. This is a brand decision, so the skill
  must ask rather than assume.
- **Mode matching**: both logos in the same mode (both knocked out or both
  full color); never convert our logo to grayscale to match.
- "Powered by" lockups and who may use them (OEM partners with agreements).
- Approval process and legal consequences of misuse.

## 10. Editorial and messaging style

The 2019 guide spends 24 pages here. Minimum viable version:

- Company and product name usage (exact capitalization, trademark symbol on
  first use only, never prefix product names with the company name).
- Preferred terms vs. banned terms (e.g., "threat" vs. "malware" vs.
  "attack"; "AI" not "AI and machine learning" in the same sentence).
- Formatting: Oxford comma, ampersand rules, dates, times, phone numbers,
  addresses, numbers and currency.
- Claims policy: avoid superlatives, cite third-party sources for any stat.
- Tone prohibitions (no fear-based selling, no attribution of attacks).
- A pointer to a base style guide (AP, Chicago, or an industry guide) for
  everything not covered.

## 11. Governance

- **Brand checklist**: yes/no questions for final review ("Is the logo the
  current version? Are we using the updated green? Are icons from the current
  set?"). The 2019 guide ends this way; it is the most practical page in it.
- **Contacts**: who approves exceptions, who supplies files.
- **Asset locations**: where the logo files, fonts, icons, and templates live.
- **Version history**.

## 12. Recurring failure patterns found in the source guides

Both professionally produced guides contain errors that undermine trust.
The skill exists partly to prevent these, which is why it validates data
before building anything.

| Failure | Example from sources | Prevention in the skill |
|---|---|---|
| HEX and RGB disagree | A swatch with HEX `#0360C5` listed as RGB `0, 128, 255`; a gray listed as RGB `70, 75, 83` beside HEX `#F1F3F7` | `validate_brand.py` derives RGB from HEX and flags mismatches |
| Copy-pasted print values | Several different blues all listed as PMS 285 and one CMYK value | Validator flags duplicate PMS/CMYK across visibly different colors |
| Malformed values | Black given as HEX `#00000` (five digits) | Validator rejects malformed HEX |
| Contradictory font names | Headline font called Montserrat, Gilroy, and Manrope on adjacent pages | Single source of truth in `brand.json`; every slide reads from it |
| Typos in the guide itself | "Montserrate," "pallet," "it's parts" | Final proofread step |
| No version or date | 2025 guide | Version and date are required fields |
| No usage ratio or contrast data | Both | Deck computes a contrast matrix and asks for a ratio |
| Stale cross-references | "Approved colors can be found on page 22" when they are on page 31 | Deck uses section names, not page numbers |
| Contradictory partner rules | Divider required vs. divider forbidden | Ask explicitly; never default |

| Personas | Skip | Optional | Optional |
| Logo system + don'ts | Required | Required | Required |

| Typography hierarchy | Basic | Required | Required |
| Imagery / icons | Optional | Required | Required |
